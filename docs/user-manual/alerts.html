Alerts

Triage what the AI escalated: review, act, note, and feed back.

When an agent's conversation suggests risk — or an automated check trips — the platform raises an alert for a human to review. Alerts is where your team triages, acts, and closes the loop.

Where alerts live now
There is no separate Alerts page. Alerts appear in Communications and on the dashboard, and open in the right-hand detail panel alongside calls, meetings and conversations. Unread alerts are shown in bold; opening one marks it read, for you only. Old links to the alerts page still work — they redirect. The description below is of the panel.

Where alerts come from

Two places, and the alert says which:

  • The agent found it (source Conversation). Every message a client sends is read by the classifier as it arrives. When it matches one of the agent's detectors that is set to raise an alert, an alert appears within about a minute — no one has to press anything. The alert is titled with the detector that fired, opens on what triggered this — one line naming the moment, usually in the client's own words — and links to the exact conversation.
  • A navigator raised it (source Default). See raising an alert yourself below.

Which detectors raise alerts is set per agent, under Agents → edit → Advanced: conversation classification. Detectors that are not set to raise still flag the conversation for review — they just don't put it in anyone's queue.

Self-harm is always on
One detector cannot be switched off: any expression of wanting to die, of self-harm, or of not wanting to go on raises a high-priority alert on every agent, whether or not anyone configured it. You can reword how it is described to the model, but not disable it or lower its priority.

What an alert shows

  • Active alerts — cards showing the client, a summary, when it was detected, its priority (urgent/high, medium, low) and status (new, in progress, resolved). Alerts from the same client are grouped.
  • Filters — by client, priority and status; sort by date; active filters are listed so you can clear them individually or all at once.
  • Resolved alerts archive — resolved alerts and false alarms move out of the active panel but stay searchable.

Reviewing an alert

  1. Open the alert. Read the summary and, when linked, jump to the exact conversation it came from.
  2. Choose an action from the panel footer: Resolve, Reopen, mark a false alarm (which resolves it into the false-alarms archive), or turn the client's agent off while you investigate and back on afterwards. The chosen action is saved to the alert log.
  3. Add an internal note — what was reviewed or decided, and optionally what changed for the client. Notes are saved to the alert log for the whole team.

Raising an alert yourself

Not every alert comes from the agent. While reading a conversation you can press Raise an alert and record what you saw — a short title, a level, and what the agent missed or what worried you. Alerts you raise are marked as raised by a navigator, so they are never confused with the ones the classifier produced.

Important
Turning the agent off is per client, not per alert — two alerts about the same person can't disagree about whether their agent is running. While it is off, the caregiver's messages still arrive and are still recorded; what stops is the agent answering them. You are asked for a reason, and who turned it off and when is kept with the client.
