Communications

One queue across all your clients: what still needs doing, and what already happened.

Communications is the page you work from. Instead of a separate list for calls, another for alerts and another for conversations, it puts everything that passed between your team and your clients into two lists, and lets you open any of it without leaving the page.

The two lists

  • Coming up — what still needs doing, soonest first: scheduled calls, in-person meetings, and anything waiting on you.
  • Happened — what already did, newest first: finished calls, chatbot conversations, alerts and recordings.

Search filters both lists at once — by client, protocol or place. Navigators see only their own clients; admins can tick Show all clients.

The detail panel

Clicking any row opens it in a panel on the right, with the list still visible beside it. This is the single biggest change in the redesign: reading an item no longer takes you to a different page and back.

Every kind of item uses the same frame, so the shape never changes:

  • a header that stays put — what this is, who it concerns, and its status;
  • a tab strip — Overview / Technical for an alert, Summary / Conversation for a chat, Protocols / Notes (and Recording, once it has ended) for a call;
  • the body, which is the only part that scrolls;
  • a footer holding the actions, always in the same place.

Actions either settle the item where it is or open a small dialog. Nothing navigates you away, so you never lose your place in the list.

Tip
The open item is part of the web address. You can bookmark a panel, reload the page and still be looking at it, open it in a new tab, or paste the link to a colleague — they will land on the same item, provided it is one of their clients.

Read and unread

Alerts and chatbot conversations arrive unread, shown in bold. Opening one marks it read. Read marks are yours alone, so a supervisor looking through the queue does not clear them for the navigator who owns the client.

Calls and meetings have no read state — a call you scheduled yourself was never news to you.

Scheduling

Use Schedule to book a call or an in-person meeting. Pick the client, whether it is a phone call or in person (and if in person, where), the meeting type and optionally the protocol to run. The platform warns you if it clashes with something already in the diary.

Cancelling and reinstating

A scheduled call can be cancelled from its panel, with a reason. It leaves the queue but stays in the history, struck through and greyed out — because "arranged and then called off" is a different fact from "never arranged", and the record should show which happened. Put it back reinstates it.

Only a call that is still pending can be cancelled. A call that already happened keeps its outcome; that is never overwritten.

Heads-up
The old Calls list page and the standalone Alerts list page no longer exist — Communications replaces both. Old links still work: they redirect here and open the right item in the panel.
