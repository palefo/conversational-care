Protocols

Design the structured question sets your team follows during calls.

Protocols are the structured question sets navigators follow during calls — onboarding interviews, follow-ups, assessments. Admins and navigators design them in a form-style editor; answers recorded during meetings are stored per client.

A protocol reaches a call in two steps. First it is added to a client's programme on their profile, which is what puts it in their call panel at all. Then a call is booked to address one or more of them, which is what lifts those to the top of the panel while the call is happening.

The protocol list

Under Settings → Protocols (or the protocol pages) each protocol shows its title and whether it is still editable or already has answers. A new protocol stays fully editable until answers are recorded against it — then it locks to protect collected data (it opens read-only).

Designing a protocol

  1. Click New protocol and give it a title (required) and an optional Markdown description of its goal.
  2. Add questions one by one. Each question is written in Markdown with a formatting toolbar (bold, italic, headings, lists, links, code) and a live Preview.
  3. Reorder by dragging, duplicate a question, or move it down; delete what you don't need.
  4. Save. The protocol can now be added to a client's programme, and attached to meetings when scheduling calls.

Choosing who a protocol applies to

Open a client and click Edit. Under Protocols, tick the ones this person works through. Only those appear in their call panel.

A new client starts with nothing ticked, so their panel is empty until somebody decides what they are on. Unticking a protocol later removes it from the panel; it never deletes answers already recorded against it, and re-ticking brings them back into view.

Protocols answered more than once

Most protocols are asked once. Some are instruments meant to be re-taken — a cognitive-decline questionnaire compares someone with how they were, so the score only means something next to the last one.

Tick This protocol is answered more than once in the editor and every call records its own round: the questions come up empty for the call you are on, with the earlier rounds listed under each one, dated and read-only. The protocol never reads as Complete — it says which round this is, or when the last one was.

This is the one setting that stays editable after a protocol locks, because you usually discover a protocol is repeatable only after using it once. It changes how answers are displayed, never what they say.

Correcting an earlier round
There is no Update button on a previous round. Open the call that round belongs to and change it there, so every round stays a record of one conversation.
Tip
Because locked protocols cannot change, duplicate an existing protocol into a new one when you need a revised version — past answers stay attached to the original.
